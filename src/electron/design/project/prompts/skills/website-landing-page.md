title: Website & landing page
description: Marketing sites, landing pages and redesigns, built to convert, in the user's brand
---
The user wants a marketing page: a website or landing page that presents a business, product, project or person and gets visitors to act. It is not an app: no login flows, dashboards, app shells or sidebars. The page's job is persuasion: a visitor understands the offer and takes the one action that matters.

Before building, ask what the brief genuinely needs to get right, in one round of questions, each with a stated default so the user can just say "go". The ones that matter most: what is the page for, and what should a visitor do on it (book, buy, sign up, get in touch, hire)? Is there anything to work from: brand assets, an existing site or screenshot, a reference they admire? One page or a small multi-page site? Ask beyond these only where the brief leaves a real gap (pricing to show, imagery, tone). If the brief already answers them, or the user doesn't want to choose, stop asking: build, and present two or three labeled visual directions.

When the user supplies an existing site or design (a URL, a screenshot, pasted HTML), treat it as a redesign and keep their real content: headlines, offerings, prices, contact details. A URL gives you the page's words (fetch it and work from that copy) but not its look, so ask for a screenshot or their brand colors instead of guessing a palette from text. From a screenshot or HTML, take the palette and type before restyling anything. Supplied material is ground truth: never replace it with invented copy, and never ignore an attached logo, brand color or reference.

Build with the anatomy of a marketing page:
- a hero that states the offer in one sentence, with one clear call to action;
- proof a visitor can trust (testimonials, client logos, numbers) taken from the user's material, or visibly marked placeholders;
- benefit sections that answer a visitor's real doubts, not a list of features;
- a footer with real navigation.
One primary action per page, repeated down the page, not three competing buttons.

Lead with real photography: on a marketing page imagery is the backbone (a full-bleed hero photo, sections that show the actual product, place or people), not decoration added at the end. Use the user's images when they have them; otherwise put a clean placeholder that says exactly what belongs there ("Photo of the storefront at dusk"), sized like the final image. A type-only page is a deliberate choice for brands where photography really doesn't fit, not the default.

On a landing page the copy is the product. Write specific copy from what the user told you: their product, their customers, their voice. Never lorem ipsum, never "Welcome to our website", never filler that could describe any business. Where a real fact is missing (a price, a date, an address), put a visibly marked placeholder like [YOUR PRICE]; don't make one up.

Make it responsive and check it before presenting: look at it at a phone width and fix what breaks (headlines that wrap badly, squashed grids, text too small to read).
